Boundary Theory

Boundary-Relative State-Time Measurement Lineage Across Nested System Boundaries

A measurement can change meaning when you redraw the boundary around a system. This paper gives a rule for checking whether a larger model still preserves what the smaller one measured instead of quietly replacing it.

EndGame

From Representation to Realization: Stage-Resolved Causal Attribution in Apparently Agentic Transitions

An outcome can look agentic without telling you which part of the process caused it. This paper breaks the path from possible action to realized outcome into separate causal questions so evidence at one stage is not mistaken for proof of the whole chain.

Time

Boundary-Relative Temporal Experience and Judgment: Process History, Represented Extent, and Query-Specific Readouts

Feeling time, judging duration, seeing a clock, and using time to act are not the same thing. This paper separates them so experiments do not treat different temporal measurements as if they were one phenomenon.

You Are Here

Bottleneck-Relative Organization of Task-Relevant Phenomenal Content: The You Are Here Hypothesis

What consciousness is about may tell us where progress is currently blocked. This paper proposes that when the limiting step in an action changes, the focal content of experience should change with it.

Paper details → ↑ Research index

Icarus

Constrained Realization: A Cross-Level Framework for the Causal Provenance of Conscious Experience

A conscious outcome can be shaped by causes at very different depths—from development and learned history to current state and immediate realization. This paper gives a way to ask where a causal difference actually entered the chain instead of treating the final report as the explanation.

Containment

Grey Matter: Where What's Real Gets Shaped Into What's Lived - A Perturbation-Response Architecture for Conscious Experience

Experience is constantly being perturbed, yet usually remains coherent. This paper turns that stability into something measurable by forcing experiments to say what changed, what property of experience was preserved, and what their measurement actually captures.

How Long Is a Thought?

How Long Is a Thought? Separating Observer-Measured Duration from Temporal Use and Reconstruction

A stopwatch can tell an observer how long a thought lasted, but that does not mean the person used time while thinking. This paper shows why measured duration, temporal awareness, and later reconstruction must be treated as different things.

Squirrel

Task-Initiation Failure as an Underidentified Endpoint: A Framework for Distinguishing Sources of the Goal-to-Action Gap

Failing to start a task does not tell you why the task did not start. This paper treats non-initiation as a shared endpoint that can come from different failures between wanting to act and actually beginning, and lays out how to tell those apart.

PhoneWalletKeys

Action-Specific History in Present Choice and Initiation: Separating Provenance Discriminability from Repeated-Action History

Knowing what you did before is different from merely recognizing what you saw, and repeated action history can shape what happens now. This paper proposes two experiments that separate self-action provenance from familiarity and accumulated repetition from the immediate action problem.

FuckYourFeelings

Emotional Reality Before Explanation: Conscious Feeling and Relevance for How to Proceed

A feeling can be real before anyone can explain why it is there. This paper separates the occurrence of conscious emotion from interpretation, accuracy, justification, and how much authority the feeling should have over what happens next.

Paper details → ↑ Research index

Push

Knowledge Handoffs Under Change: A Structured Assessment Design for Adaptive Revision

A person can inherit the right answer without inheriting why it is right, when it stops applying, or how to revise it. This paper tests what a knowledge handoff must preserve so a recipient can keep, limit, or re-derive an answer when conditions change.

Pull

When an Alternative Becomes Possible: Lived Reorganization and Action-Option Generation Under a Common Perturbation History

Advice can be understood long before an alternative becomes a live option. This paper asks whether a change in how a situation is experienced and the ability to generate a new action are one transition or two separable responses to the same history.

Paper details → ↑ Research index

Weight

Weight: Why the Same Words Do Not Carry the Same Consequence

Two people can understand and agree with the same words while those words carry very different consequences for attention, belief, memory, valuation, or action. This paper proposes a disciplined way to name and measure that difference without treating Weight as a universal mechanism.

Paper details → ↑ Research index

Relevance

What Does It Mean for the Past to Still Matter? A Historical-Relevance Audit of Causal Persistence, Information, and Control

The past can still matter in several different ways: it may still cause a present outcome, leave information available now, change what should be done, or actually control what happens. This paper separates those relations so evidence for one is not mistaken for proof of the others.
