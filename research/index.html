Six research articles are currently under consideration at Systems Research and Behavioral Science.

Under review · Systems Research and Behavioral Science

Boundary-Relative State-Time Measurement Lineage Across Nested System Boundaries

Introduces a systems-measurement framework for accumulated histories defined relative to explicit analytical boundaries. It focuses on preserving measurement lineage when a system boundary is enlarged, so an expanded model remains a genuine extension rather than silently replacing the original measurement.

In screening · Systems Research and Behavioral Science

Factorization-Sensitive Non-Identifiability in Endpoint Control

Shows that endpoint-control measures can preserve overall causal capacity while losing the temporal factorization needed to determine which earlier intervention coordinates still matter. The paper establishes the limitation constructively for deterministic and stochastic systems.

Under review · Systems Research and Behavioral Science

From Representation to Realization: Stage-Resolved Causal Attribution in Apparently Agentic Transitions

Develops a stage-resolved method for causal attribution across candidate participation, selection, implementation, and realized outcomes. Stronger end-to-end attribution is warranted only when the necessary causal evidence remains valid across each stage.

Under review · Systems Research and Behavioral Science

Temporal Self-Location in Bounded Cognitive Episodes: Separating Process History from Elapsed-Time Access and Control

Separates the fact that a cognitive episode has an evolving history from whether it has usable information about its own elapsed position in time. It also distinguishes access to temporal information from whether time has authority to influence continuation or termination of the episode.

Under review · Systems Research and Behavioral Science

Boundary-Relative Temporal Experience and Judgment: Process History, Represented Extent, and Query-Specific Readouts

Separates processing history, represented temporal extent, duration judgment, felt passage, and clock-linked action rather than treating them as one subjective-time variable. It proposes a systems methodology for preserving the meaning of those measurements as information sources and analytical boundaries change.

In screening · Systems Research and Behavioral Science

Bottleneck-Relative Organization of Task-Relevant Phenomenal Content: The You Are Here Hypothesis

Proposes that, within a broadly common action architecture, the functional location of the transition currently limiting progress predicts what the focal experienced problem is about. The paper turns that correspondence into a testable hypothesis while separating it from generic difficulty, effort, conflict, and semantic reconstruction.
