When an Alternative Becomes Possible: Lived Reorganization and Action-Option Generation Under a Common Perturbation History

Download manuscript PDF →Download companion PDF →

Abstract

People can report that advice or information “finally made sense” despite earlier understanding; at other times, they can name an alternative while reporting that the situation feels unchanged. Existing work already separates supplied possibilities from generated options, links problem representation to option generation, and studies coherence among experiential, behavioral, and physiological responses under common elicitation. The present article asks a narrower identification question: under prospectively factorized histories of environmental evidence, can a report-derived representation of task-relevant relational/meaning organization and standardized first-production of a prelearned target action be predicted adequately by one preregistered shared response coordinate? Hidden-Reroute varies cue diagnosticity and repetition without naming the target alternative. After matched evidence histories, separate trials sample immediate open reports, standardized option generation, explicit environmental belief, generic-state comparators, or reconstruction controls. Primary Φ excludes explicit option/action identity, and prior probe history is modeled because repeated measurement can alter later responses. Analysis first tests current-evidence sufficiency and a flexible shared-coordinate model class, then asks whether richer global response geometry is required. Because primary Φ may contain several frozen contrasts, global richer geometry is not by itself evidence of Φ/G separation. A prospectively frozen attribution gate first estimates the intervention-response structure required by Φ alone, then tests whether G is adequately contained within that structure or adds material held-out predictive geometry beyond it. The proposal therefore tests a bounded measurement architecture, not a psychological mechanism. It does not infer simultaneous within-episode change, causal direction between Φ and G, latent candidacy, or ontological dimensionality.

In plain language

A person can understand advice, repeat it accurately, and even agree with it while the alternative it describes still does not appear as a live way forward. Later, the words may be unchanged but the situation is experienced differently and the alternative becomes something the person can actually generate.

The paper separates two responses to the same controlled history: how the situation is organized in immediate lived experience and whether a prelearned alternative is produced under a standardized option-generation prompt. It then asks whether both responses can be represented adequately by one shared response coordinate.

Why it matters

Phrases such as “it finally clicked” or “now I can see another way” can refer to several different changes. They may describe semantic understanding, a change in the meaning of the situation, generation of an option, preference, intention, or later action. Treating them as one transition makes the claim difficult to test.

The proposed design avoids assuming that experience causes option generation or that option generation causes experience. It measures them on separate branches under matched perturbation histories and tests whether their response structure can be compressed together without losing predictive information.

If one response requires structure the other does not, the result would justify keeping lived reorganization and action-option generation scientifically separate. If one shared coordinate predicts both adequately, the stronger distinction should contract.
