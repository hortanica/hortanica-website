What this paper is about

The paper examines a measurement problem that appears whenever the boundary used to define a system changes. A variable that looks external at one analytical boundary can become part of the system state at a larger boundary, changing what can be measured, attributed, or treated as causally sufficient.

In the temporal case, the focus is not simply on whether a system has a clock. The question is whether the lineage of the measurement remains identifiable as the system boundary changes.

Why it matters

The broader implication is methodological: claims about state, time, and causal attribution can depend on what the analysis treats as inside or outside the system. Expanding a boundary is therefore useful only when it exposes a variable, intervention, or dependency that was previously hidden and changes an empirical prediction or attribution.
