Research

Following a State Through a System

This research program examines how complex systems are described, how present states are constructed, how those states become action, and how the consequences of action alter what happens next.

The work began with a recurring identification problem: the same observed outcome can support very different explanations depending on where the system boundary is drawn, what history is preserved, which relation is being measured, and what information is actually available to the system.

To make that path tractable, I organize the work into four analytical phases: Foundation, Construction, Action, and Integration. These phases are a working boundary for this research program, not a claim that cognition, behavior, or complex systems universally divide into four natural stages. They provide a way to trace a state through different kinds of questions while keeping the relevant evidence and attribution limits explicit.

The organization is directional, but not one-way. Work later in the path can change what must be asked earlier. Integration can alter the system that generates the next state and can therefore force boundaries, measurement objects, attribution rules, or distinctions in Foundation to be reconsidered.

Four analytical phases

A working decomposition for tracing the process

Phase 01

Foundation

What are we actually entitled to say about this state?

Foundation establishes the rules needed before stronger explanations of a state can be trusted. It asks what counts as the system, what is actually being measured, what an observation can and cannot identify, where causal provenance resides, when distinct relations must remain separate, when evidence can be composed, and when a distinction should collapse. Its role is to keep later explanations from claiming more than the evidence supports.

Phase 02

Construction

Why is the system in this state now rather than another?

Construction asks how the present state gets produced. It follows how history, physiology, context, memory, affect, valuation, information, learned structure, environmental conditions, and current functional organization contribute to what is presently experienced, represented, prioritized, or made available for action. The phase remains open-ended: each paper isolates part of the construction problem without assuming that the current set forms a complete model.

Phase 03

Action

How does a constructed state become consequence?

Action asks how a constructed and action-ready state becomes something that happens in the world. It follows selection, implementation, expression, speech, movement, overt choice, sustained behavior, social interaction, and environmental consequence while keeping those transitions distinct enough to identify where realization succeeds, fails, redirects, or depends on other systems.

Explore Phase 3 →

Phase 04

Integration

What does that consequence change about the system that comes next?

Integration closes the loop by asking what consequences change about the system that will generate the next state. It examines how outcomes become retained history, learning, revised valuation, changed context, altered structure, new constraints, or changed possibilities—and how those changes can force earlier assumptions, boundaries, measurements, or distinctions to be revisited.
