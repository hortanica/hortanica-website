Research

Decomposing the Human Experience

This research program examines the human experience by separating processes that are often treated as though they were the same thing.

What we feel, what we know, what becomes available to us, what influences us, what we choose, what we do, and what happens afterward are closely related—but they are not necessarily identical.

The work asks how those pieces are formed, how they interact, and what can actually be concluded from the behavior and experience we observe.

The research is organized into four phases: Foundation, Construction, Action, and Integration. These phases are not proposed stages of thought or behavior. They separate different questions within the larger problem of understanding how a human being is shaped, experiences the world, acts within it, and is changed by what happens next.

Phase 01 — Foundation

How your system works for you.

Foundation looks at the boundaries, constraints, and relationships that make your experience possible in the first place.

It asks what belongs inside the system being examined, what comes from outside it, what information can be carried forward, what gets lost, and what changes when those boundaries move. It also separates things that can look like one process from things that actually do different jobs.

The goal is to understand the structure before asking how it produces the world you experience.

Phase 02 — Construction

How your reality is built and shaped.

By the time you experience the world, it has already been shaped by more than what is happening immediately around you.

Your body, history, memory, expectations, emotions, environment, relationships, learned patterns, and current condition can all influence what becomes noticeable, meaningful, threatening, desirable, familiar, or possible.

Construction examines how those influences come together to shape the reality available to you—and why the same world may not become the same experience for everyone, or even for you at a different time.

Explore Phase 2 →

Phase 03 — Action

How your experience becomes your actions.

Experiencing something is not the same as acting on it.

A feeling can be present without being followed. An option can exist without being chosen. Something can matter without deciding what happens next. And what you ultimately do can depend on processes that are not all visible from inside the moment.

Action examines what happens between experience and behavior: how possibilities gain influence, compete, become selected, are expressed, stopped, redirected, or carried through into something the world can respond to.

It is the point where what happens within your experience becomes part of what others can experience as you.

Explore Phase 3 →

Phase 04 — Integration

How you use the experience for next time.

What happens to you does not end with the moment.

Experience and action can change what you remember, expect, notice, value, avoid, repeat, trust, fear, or consider possible. They can alter your relationships and surroundings as well, changing the conditions you will encounter later.

Integration asks what you carry forward, how it becomes part of what shapes your next moment, and how what you have lived through can change what becomes possible the next time around.
